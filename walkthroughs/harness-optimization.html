Paper Walkthrough: Harness Optimization

A 45–60 minute team session guide. Every number and equation below is taken from the tracker's tutorials, which are verified against the papers. Links go to the full tutorials.

Why this session

The harness — the scaffold of prompts, tools, memory, and control flow around a frozen model — has stopped being plumbing and become the research object of 2026. Three forces converged: (1) harness choice now swings agent performance more than model choice (swapping harnesses moves SWE-bench Pro pass@1 from 23% to 52% on the same model); (2) harness execution traces are being framed as the next training corpus — Jianfeng Gao's Berkeley AI Summit pitch was literally "harnesses are the new training data"; and (3) a wave of papers now treats the harness as something to optimize, evolve, or train against rather than hand-author. This session maps that wave — and the counter-wave showing that many "harness evolution" gains are repeated sampling, judge-pleasing, or overfitting in disguise. Our job: find the real signal and decide what to build on it.

The landscape in one picture

The field splits into three sub-threads. (A) treats the harness itself as the optimization variable (model frozen). (B) treats the model as the optimization variable, trained in or for a harness. (C) asks whether the gains from either are real, transferable, and worth their cost.

Thread A is the H-half, Thread B the π-half, Thread C the referee. The Self-Improvement survey (Schmidhuber senior author) adds a telling statistic: 166 of its 239 papers update the scaffold rather than the weights — the field's center of gravity is already here.

Part A: Optimizing the harness (20 min)

1. Recursive Harness Self-Improvement (RHI) — tutorial

Core idea. If the harness is expressed as text — the whole agent loop as a prompt-level specification (roles, contracts, hops) — harness optimization becomes a text-refinement problem the model can do to itself: run the harness, compare each revision's output against its predecessor's via an LLM judge, rewrite the spec conditioned on the accumulated preference history. The authors argue the gains come from better task-specific context management (which information reaches which sub-agent), not longer reasoning.

The number. On 30 synthetic ML-research tasks, a few RHI iterations let a low-reasoning-effort agent beat the same model at maximum reasoning effort, at up to 60% lower inference cost.

The mechanism. Instead of the intractable population objective (win rate against all competing harnesses, Θ(m²) comparisons), RHI uses a trajectory-local relaxation — compare only against the immediately previous revision, Θ(1) per iteration:

\[\widetilde f_x^{(i)}(H)=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{A}(H,x),\; y^{-}\sim\mathcal{A}(H_x^{(i-1)},x)}\big[\mathbf{1}\{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eval}}(y,y^{-};x_{\mathrm{eval}})=y\succ y^{-}\}\big], \qquad H_x^{(i+1)}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{harness}}\big(H_x^{(i)},\mathcal{D}_x^{(i)}\big)\]

where \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eval}}\) is an LLM judge and \(\mathcal{D}_x^{(i)}\) the self-comparison history; under a Bradley–Terry latent-utility model each comparison is a noisy local-ascent signal.

Discussion question. RHI's judge is an LLM comparing the harness's own outputs — this is exactly the loop Part C says can be Goodharted. What external evidence would convince us an RHI-style loop improved the harness rather than trained it to please the judge?

2. Living-Harness — tutorial

Core idea. The most disciplined evolve-the-harness system so far: after each evaluated episode, a frozen per-domain Evolution-SOP converts trajectory + evaluator signals into candidate repairs, which must pass five commit gates (schema, scope, evidence, constraint, merge) before landing in exactly two evolving stores — episodic memory (trigger/failure/recovery) and a state graph (repair edges). Tools, context, weights, and the SOP itself stay frozen; episodes are scored before their evidence can be committed.

The number. With GPT-5.2-medium: 83.09 average Pass@1 on τ²-Bench (+10.07pp over Reflexion, above Gemini 3 Pro's 82.92) — and ablating the Evolution-SOP while keeping the memory/graph containers costs 9.7 points, so structured bounded evolution, not memory per se, is the active ingredient.

The mechanism. The bounded-update decomposition:

\[H_{d}^{(n)}=\left(C_{d}^{\mathrm{act}},\;\psi_{d},\;S_{d}^{(n)}\right),\qquad S_{d}^{(n)}=\left(\mathcal{R}_{d}^{(n)},\,G_{d}^{(n)}\right)\]

frozen actor context \(C\), frozen Evolution-SOP \(\psi\), and only the state \(S\) (memory \(\mathcal{R}\) + graph \(G\)) evolves. The killer result: the evolved state is portable capital — frozen and reused retrieval-only, it improves four other backbones on every reported domain (MultiWOZ Taxi from 0.00 to ~43–45 for GLM-5/Qwen3-max/Kimi-k2).

Discussion question. The gates prevent noise ingestion but there's no rollback or re-audit of old entries — a plausible-but-wrong repair is retrieved forever. What would a regression-testing discipline for harness state look like?

3. EvoHarness-RL — tutorial (the bridge from A to B)

Core idea. Everyone bolts memory/state/skill stores onto agents; nobody trains the policy for when touching them is worth a turn. EvoHarness-RL abstracts the harness into three policy-facing states — Belief, Progress, Experience — accessed via four meta-actions (track, commit, recall, note) that share the environment's action budget, then trains harness usage with SFT + cost-aware GRPO.

The number. ALFWorld with Qwen3-8B: ReAct 47.9% → 96.9% on the seen split (matching Claude Opus 4.5's ReAct at 96.4%), 86.6% unseen.

The mechanism. The cost-aware reward — a dominant sparse success term plus shaping for adaptive harness use:

\[ R(\tau) = R_{\mathrm{succ}}(\tau) + \lambda_{\mathrm{eff}} R_{\mathrm{eff}}(\tau) + \lambda_{\mathrm{div}}(u)\, R_{\mathrm{div}}(\tau) - \lambda_{\mathrm{spam}} R_{\mathrm{spam}}(\tau) - \lambda_{\mathrm{inv}} R_{\mathrm{inv}}(\tau) \]

with efficiency bonus granted only on success and a cosine-annealed diversity bonus. The generalizable finding is harness annealing: calls drop from frequent scaffolding to ~1/episode as routine harness behavior gets internalized into weights — direct evidence that the scaffold/capability boundary moves under RL, which is the co-evolution thesis made empirical.

Discussion question. Annealing looks clean on ALFWorld, where the environment state plausibly fits in an 8B model. Where should annealing plateau on SWE-scale tasks, and could "calls per episode" become our diagnostic for what belongs in weights vs. scaffold?

4. Harness Handbook — tutorial

Core idea. RHI only works because its harness is a prompt; production harnesses are large codebases where one runtime behavior is scattered across distant files and shared state. The Handbook identifies behavior localization as the bottleneck of harness evolution and builds a behavior-centric index of the repo (static analysis + LLM structuring, auto-updated after edits), plus Behavior-Guided Progressive Disclosure to walk agents from behavior description to verified edit sites.

The number. Handbook-assisted planning raises plan win rates by +10.0 (Codex) and +18.9 (Terminus-2) points while cutting planner tokens 12.7%/8.6% — better localization and cheaper planning simultaneously.

The mechanism. Plan quality is a weighted judge score, localization dominant:

\[S = 0.5\,S_{\mathrm{Loc}} + 0.25\,S_{\mathrm{Scope}} + 0.25\,S_{\mathrm{Reason}}\]

Caveat to say out loud: plan win rate is a proxy — no end-to-end did-the-edit-pass-tests numbers in the sources.

Discussion question. If harness choice must be re-tuned per model–task pair (Part C), edit frequency goes up and this infrastructure gets more valuable. Should we build a handbook for our harness before attempting any self-modification loop on it?

5. The skills line: MSCE — tutorial

Core idea. Skills/memory are the harness's accumulating state, so governing them is harness optimization. MSCE's diagnosis: retrieved traces are passive — the frozen model must re-extract the lesson every time. Its fix is training-free governance: crystallize procedural policies into callable skills only when evidence shows positive counterfactual gain, with each skill carrying evidence links, applicability boundaries, verification rules, and a reliability estimate.

The number. Honest flag: no quantitative results survived into our verified sources (claims of outperforming skill/memory baselines on EvoAgentBench and LoCoMo are unverified) — this is the thinnest tutorial in the set; quote the mechanism, not a number.

The mechanism. Reflection-weighted value backfilling — a TD-style backup with self-reflection standing in for a critic — plus the crystallization gate:

\[V(f_{i,t}^{(1)})=\alpha_{i,t}R_{i}+(1-\alpha_{i,t})\,\gamma\,V(f_{i,t+1}^{(1)}),\qquad G(f^{(2)})=\bar{V}_{\mathrm{with}}-\bar{V}_{\mathrm{blend}}(S_{\mathrm{without}})>0\]

Discussion question. MSCE's epistemics bottom out in self-assessment (reflection weights, LLM gain estimates). Given Part C's "Agent Skills Can Be Harmful" result, what differential test should gate any skill our agents crystallize?

Two more Thread-A results worth one slide each. Harness-R1 trains a dedicated 9B "harness engineer" with GRPO whose only reward is the realized success delta from rerunning a frozen agent under the patch: +9.3 points average (44.3%→53.6%), beating every prompted frontier editor, with gains transferring to 20 unseen target models (+7.06) — outcome-grounded training beats scale at harness editing. HarnessCompass is the process-discipline counterpart: a generalization gate (no task IDs, test names, or token-triggered branches) plus grounded agent self-reports and a component-wise merge lift SWE-bench Verified held-out Pass@1 from 51.6% to 60.4% (prior SOTA: 54.7%) in 5 iterations instead of ~20 — and the ablation shows the gate alone carries most of the transfer, while richer feedback hurts generalization until the merge contains it.

Part B: Training into the harness (15 min)

6. OpenForgeRL / Training Agents Inside Their Harnesses — tutorial

Core idea. Deployed agents live inside stateful, multi-process harnesses that open RL stacks can't express, so people train harness-free and hope behavior transfers. OpenForgeRL inverts control: the harness runs unmodified in its own container, and a lightweight proxy serves its model calls from the current policy while recording them as training data for a standard RL stack (veRL + GRPO) — harness complexity becomes invisible to the trainer.

The number. With only hundreds to a few thousand tasks, OpenForgeGUI hits 72.3 WebVoyager / 63.0 Online-Mind2Web / 37.7 OSWorld-Verified, beating same-size open baselines nearly everywhere — and the qualitative headline: harness choice is a training variable (some harnesses are substantially harder to learn in than others), while error recovery stays weak even after RL.

The mechanism. The policy never sees raw state — only the harness-constructed context — and credit is broadcast terminal reward:

\[\tau=\big\langle(\mathcal{H}(s_{0}),a_{0}),\ldots,(\mathcal{H}(s_{T}),a_{T})\big\rangle,\qquad r_{t}=\gamma^{\,T-t}\cdot r_{T},\ \ \gamma=1.0\]

i.e., every model call in a successful rollout gets full terminal reward — which is precisely the crudeness TRACE and AgentOPSD attack.

Discussion question. If harness choice shapes what RL can learn, do we co-design the harness for trainability — and is "easy to learn in" the same property as "good at inference time"?

7. TRACE — tutorial

Core idea. One terminal scalar smeared over 60 tool calls is why long-horizon agentic RL is sample-starved. TRACE gets dense turn-level credit with no critic, no process labels, no MC rollouts: a frozen reference model scores "answer readiness" — the gold answer's log-likelihood given each trajectory prefix — and one-step temporal differences of that value become per-turn rewards that telescope, so redundant tool calls can't farm credit.

The number. Pure RL from base models: Qwen3-4B 7.2 → 35.6 and Qwen3-30B-A3B 8.4 → 42.6 on BrowseComp-Plus, beating GRPO/GSPO/GiGPO (+5–6 points) under matched data/tools/rewards.

The mechanism.

\[V(S_{k})=\log\frac{d_{0}}{d_{k}}=\log\frac{-\bar{\ell}_{0}+\epsilon}{-\bar{\ell}_{k}+\epsilon},\qquad \delta_{k}=V(S_{k+1})-V(S_{k})\]

where \(\bar\ell_k\) is the frozen reference's mean log-prob of the gold answer after \(k\) turns and \(d_k\) the remaining uncertainty gap. Scope is genuinely narrow — requires a short verifiable gold answer.

Discussion question. EvoHarness-RL's harness meta-actions are exactly the turns whose marginal value TRACE could price. Does a TRACE-style signal replace hand-built harness-usage shaping — and what's the analogue when the "answer" is a passing test suite?

8. AgentOPSD — tutorial

Core idea. The distillation-flavored answer to the same credit problem: the teacher is the policy itself conditioned on a training-only retrieved skill. Token-level privileged-vs-plain log-prob gaps aggregate into turn evidence, a decaying Bayesian log-odds filter (anchored at the group success rate) turns evidence into marginal belief revisions, and GRPO's sequence advantage is reweighted per turn — no critic, no extra rollouts, skills never used at inference.

The number. 89.1% ALFWorld success at 7B (GRPO: 81.2); at 3B, Search-QA 36.4 → 46.7 and WebShop score 79.8 → 90.4 — with ablations attributing gains to the credit construction, not the privileged access.

The mechanism. Turn evidence as a self-teacher likelihood ratio:

\[e_{k}=\sum_{t=1}^{L_{k}}\Big[\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{k,t}\mid h_{k,t}^{+})-\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{k,t}\mid h_{k,t})\Big] =\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(a_{k}\mid s_{k},c^{+})}{\pi_{\theta}(a_{k}\mid s_{k})}\]

fed through \(B_k=\sigma(\operatorname{logit}(B_0)+\sum_j \gamma^{k-j}e_j)\), credit \(\Delta B_k\).

Discussion question. Here a skill library's third use appears: privileged training-time context rather than inference scaffolding. Should our skill libraries be built for training-time consumption from day one?

Industry signal (one line): Liquid AI reports a four-stage post-training recipe ending in multi-turn agentic RL run through Pi, Hermes Agent, and OpenClaw with sandboxed rollouts — harness-native training is already a production recipe, not just a research bet.

Part C: Does it actually work? (15 min)

Frame this section as red-team questions aimed back at Parts A and B.

9. No Universally Best Harness — tutorial

Core idea. Decompose OpenEvolve/TTT-Discover-style discovery harnesses into 30 budget-matched variants, run them across 12 model–problem pairs with 3.1M+ rollouts and real hypothesis testing. Result: no fixed harness is reliably superior; complex machinery generally underperforms simpler alternatives; harness choice is a model–problem-specific hyperparameter. Constructively: early progress predicts final performance, so prune-and-reallocate across harnesses beats committing to any fixed one.

The number. The strongest fixed harness reaches cross-pair majority-win \(\widehat P_{\mathrm{maj}}=0.914\) with raw \(p=0.023\) — which rises to 0.678 after Holm correction: not even the best harness survives multiple-comparison correction.

The mechanism.

\[\widehat{P}_{\mathrm{maj}}(h) = \frac{1}{R} \sum_{r=1}^{R} \mathbf{1}\Big\{ \sum_{j=1}^{12} \mathbf{1}\{M^{h}_{j} > M^{0,r}_{j}\} \geq 7 \Big\}\]

Red-team question for Part A. Every Thread-A paper reports one model on one benchmark family. Which of RHI / Living-Harness / HarnessCompass would survive this paper's 12-pair, repeated-trial, Holm-corrected protocol? (HarnessCompass's cross-model transfer is the only direct attempt.)

10. Rethinking Harness Evolution (tracked, no tutorial yet — blog + code)

Core idea. Self-evolving harness loops search directly on the test set, which quietly makes them a form of test-time scaling. Under matched feedback and inference budgets against four conditions (parallel sampling, sequential refinement, shared harness evolution, per-instance harness scaling), harness evolution fails to beat plain parallel sampling; its gains show up at pass@5 but barely at pass@1 (multiple attempts, not a better harness); and on a disjoint search/eval split the evolved harness transfers almost nothing — even though the budget accounting favors it (harness-generation compute is uncounted). The tracked "Harness Delta Attribution" blog extends this with a decomposition: harness evolution takes a 0.8B model to 100% on ALFWorld, but across 4 benchmarks much of the gain attributes to overfitting or extra compute rather than generalizable design.

Red-team question for Part A. Adopt this as a house rule? No harness-evolution claim without a matched-budget repeated-sampling baseline, pass@1 (not pass@k) deltas, and a disjoint search/eval split. Note this is exactly what "Sample More, Reflect Less" (tutorial) found one level down: at matched token cost, 0 of 36 self-refine/reflect comparisons beat repeated sampling, and 10 were significantly worse.

11. Harness-IF — tutorial

Core idea. When an agent does what a harness rule says, did the rule cause it? Harness-IF scores 642 atomic rules across five instruction surfaces (system prompt, project file, user turn, tool description, skill description), with a zero-injection probe labeling which rules oppose the model's unprompted default. Only against-prior compliance is real steerability.

The number. All 12 frontier models drop when coincidence is stripped: raw Acc 72.1–85.9% vs. AP-Acc 66.1–78.6%, an inflation of 3.6–7.4 points with a twofold spread across models — leaderboards partially rank agreeable defaults. Bonus: in conflicts, system prompt / project file / user instruction tie exactly for first (mean rank 2.22) while tool (3.78) and skill descriptions (4.56) reliably lose; 77.1% of failures are omissions of demanded behavior, not overstepping.

The mechanism.

\[\mathrm{AP\mbox{-}Acc}(a)=\frac{\sum_{(i,r)\in\mathcal{E}_a,\ r\in\mathcal{P}} z_{a,i,r}}{|\{(i,r)\in\mathcal{E}_a: r\in\mathcal{P}\}|}\]

Red-team question. Every evolved harness in Part A accretes rules. How many of our harness rules are align-prior — i.e., decorative? An against-prior audit is a principled pruning criterion.

12. Agent Skills Can Be Harmful — tutorial

Core idea. Differential testing (same task/verifier/model, only the skill varies) surfaces 307 confirmed skill-induced failures. The shape is adversarial: the dangerous skill is not the irrelevant one but the topically relevant one whose examples and defaults quietly override task requirements — so better retrieval doesn't fix it.

The number. 86/125 functional failures (68.8%) are Task-Implementation Faults from relevant skills (only 2/125 from misapplied ones); 62.6% of efficiency regressions come from skills making verification/pipelines mandatory, not from prompt length.

The mechanism. The conservative regression gate — both cost metrics up, one more than doubled:

\[\min(r_{\mathrm{tok}},r_{\mathrm{time}})>1.0\;\land\;\max(r_{\mathrm{tok}},r_{\mathrm{time}})>2.0\]

Red-team question for MSCE / Living-Harness / any skill-crystallizing loop. Auto-distilled skills inherit every failure mode here with no human author to scope them. Should this paper's paired no-skill/matched-skill audit be the acceptance gate for anything our agents write into their own libraries?

13. Prompt-Induced Waste — tutorial

Core idea. A preregistered benchmark (4,644 runs, 24 deterministic tasks, six ≥500B-class models, two real harnesses) showing prompts allocate work, not just meaning: "develop several approaches" buys 2.4–7.4x reasoning tokens and ~3 discarded branches; "be absolutely certain" buys verification loops up to 18.25x cost — all with zero success gain, while a length-matched restatement costs ~1.0x.

The number. The harness result matters most for this session: Claude Code vs. PI.DEV differ 12–15x in static prefix, 2–7x in turns, and 5–30x in cost-per-success at equal correctness — the harness effect dwarfs any prompt effect in the study.

The mechanism. The paired reasoning ratio, each run against its own block's baseline median:

\[ R_{\text{run}} = \frac{\text{reasoning tokens}(\text{run})}{\operatorname{median}\big(\text{baseline reasoning tokens in the same model–harness–task block}\big)} \]

Red-team question. None of Part A's objectives price cost-per-success. If a harness optimizer is rewarded on success alone, it will happily evolve 18x verification loops. What does a cost-aware \(f_x(H)\) look like?

Three more skeptic results, one line each. Lost in Compaction (tutorial): harness compaction silently drops session-scoped standing rules — average 17% retention, with a separate 9B extractor pass recovering 90–96%; an evolving harness that compacts is evolving away its own constraints. Harness Rankings Are Model-Dependent (tracked, no tutorial): 10 harnesses × 2 models on SWE-bench Pro yields rank correlation −0.05 between the two models' harness leaderboards — there is no harness leaderboard, only (model, harness) pairs. Model or Harness (tracked, no tutorial): 41 agent failure modes assigned to component-interaction edges, LLM-judgeable at κ=0.76 — the shared vocabulary for whether a fix belongs in π_θ or H.

The through-line

What the evidence supports today is narrower and more interesting than the hype: harness optimization is real but local, and the burden of proof has shifted to transfer. Bounded, evidence-gated adaptation demonstrably works — Living-Harness's gated state (+10pp, portable across four backbones), Harness-R1's outcome-trained editor (+9.3, transferring to 20 unseen targets), HarnessCompass's admissibility gate (+5.7 over SOTA on held-out tasks) — and training the model in/for the harness works when credit assignment is dense (TRACE, AgentOPSD) or the infrastructure is honest about deployment (OpenForgeRL). But the skeptics have shown that unconstrained harness evolution largely rediscovers repeated sampling; that no fixed harness survives statistical correction across model–problem pairs; that measured compliance is partly agreeable defaults; and that relevant skills and innocuous phrasing silently tax or corrupt execution. The pattern in every positive result is the same: the discipline of the loop — gates, external evidence, matched budgets, differential audits — is worth more than the search machinery, which is exactly the Reliable Self-Evolving Agents survey's principle: no update should control the only evidence used for its own acceptance.

Open problems for us

  • Close the co-evolution loop nobody has closed. Nobody has shown a model trained on optimized-harness traces beating one trained on default-harness traces. This is a runnable experiment (an RHI-style loop inside OpenForgeRL rollouts) and the load-bearing claim of the whole "harnesses are the new training data" thesis.
  • A cost-aware harness objective. Every Thread-A objective optimizes success; Prompt-Induced Waste shows harness mechanics swing cost-per-success 5–30x. Define and optimize \(f(H)\) = success per dollar, with the two waste carriers (token-borne branching, tool-borne verification loops) as regularizers.
  • The evaluation standard, packaged. One harness-evolution eval suite: matched-budget parallel-sampling baseline, pass@1 on disjoint search/eval splits, Holm-corrected multi-pair testing (the No-Universal-Harness null distributions are released), plus Harness-Delta-style attribution into generalization/overfitting/compute. Publishing this would discipline the field and de-risk our own claims.
  • Trained engineer + generalization gate. Harness-R1 shows outcome-trained editing beats prompted frontier editing; HarnessCompass shows gated prompted editing beats ungated. The obvious untested composition: a GRPO-trained harness engineer whose edit space is constrained by a HarnessCompass-style admissibility gate — does it dominate both?
  • Differential skill acceptance in the loop. Wire the Agent-Skills-Harmful audit (no-skill/matched-skill pairs + the min>1/max>2 cost gate) as the commit gate for MSCE/Living-Harness-style crystallization, and measure how much garbage it rejects from a live self-evolving library.
  • Harness annealing as a measurement instrument. EvoHarness-RL's calls-per-episode annealing curve is a live probe of what's migrating from scaffold to weights. Can we use it to decide which harness features to retire per model generation — operationalizing the survey's co-evolution ratchet?
  • Credit assignment for harness edits, not just turns. TRACE prices turns against a gold answer; Harness-R1 prices whole patches by batch rerun. Nothing prices an individual harness edit on held-out tasks cheaply. A TRACE-style prefix-value signal over harness revisions would make RHI-style loops trainable rather than judged.
